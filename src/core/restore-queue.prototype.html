Question: does this model of the restore queue behave right? It covers each item's states, how installs Cairn sees in the browser are matched to items (including matches across Firefox/Chromium and within one family), updates and Chrome Sync installs, Cairn's background worker being stopped and restarted, the restore source changing on a new Pull, and the enabled-state step afterwards.

The bet: the queue never trusts individual browser events. Any event, or a wake-up after the worker was stopped, makes Cairn re-read the browser's full extension list and reconcile against it. So an update can't be mistaken for an install, a Chrome Sync install is just another observed install, and nothing is lost while the worker sleeps. The whole queue is plain data that is saved after every change.
